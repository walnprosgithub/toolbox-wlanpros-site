Wi-Fi Channel Allocation Charts

Four charts, free and print-ready. Corrected and current as of July 2026.

  • 2.4 GHz Channel Allocations (PDF) Wi-Fi, 802.15.4 / Zigbee, BLE and Bluetooth channels on one page, with guard bands and center frequencies.
  • 5 GHz Channel Allocations (PDF) U-NII-1 through U-NII-4, with FCC, ISED, ACMA and ETSI power limits and DFS requirements side by side.
  • 6 GHz Channel Allocations (PDF) Low Power Indoor, Standard Power and Very Low Power across U-NII-5 through U-NII-8.
  • 6 GHz Channel Allocations, with Geofenced Variable Power (PDF) The same chart plus the fourth United States power class, which takes effect 27 April 2026.

Last updated 29 July 2026.

What changed in this release

The corrections fall into two kinds, and the difference matters more than the corrections do.

Some were right the day they were drawn, and the regulation moved. The 6 GHz chart labeled Very Low Power as still under FCC review. FCC 24-125 extended Very Low Power into U-NII-6 and U-NII-8, released in December 2024 and effective 5 May 2025. On the 5 GHz chart, U-NII-4 was captioned "Not Currently Available For Unlicensed"; 5.850 to 5.895 GHz opened for unlicensed indoor use on 2 July 2021.

The rest were wrong from the first draft. Every 6 GHz power block carried the same channel quantity ladder. Low Power Indoor earns those numbers; Standard Power never reached the whole band, and its ladder is 41, 20, 9, 4, 1.

There are two 6 GHz charts on purpose. One shows the power classes you can buy hardware for today. The other adds Geofenced Variable Power, which takes effect 27 April 2026, and which no approved geofencing system or certified hardware exists for yet. Use whichever fits the conversation you are having.

Using these charts

They are free to download, print, laminate and hand to a customer. If you put one into a document of your own, keep the attribution on it, and check back here before you reprint. A chart on a wall has no way to tell you the world changed after it came out of the printer.
